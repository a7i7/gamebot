# Gamebot Arena — Marketing Plan: First 100 Users

## Context

Pure user-acquisition playbook. **Target:** CS students / beginners. **Budget:** ~$0. **Goal:** first ~100 real users in ~90 days. No technology, deployment, or feature discussion — just where to go, what to say, in what order, and how to know it's working.

**The one marketing truth that shapes everything:** beginners don't get excited by "a TicTacToe bot platform." They get excited by **a personal challenge with a public scoreboard and a name to beat.** Every tactic below sells the *contest and the bragging*, not the software. Your job in each channel is to bait a "I bet I can beat that bot" reflex.

---

## The Core Message (use this everywhere)

Don't say: *"A platform to write game-playing bots in Python/JS/Java/C++."*
Say: **"Can you write code that beats my bot? Climb the leaderboard."**

Three reusable hooks, ranked by pull for beginners:
1. **The dare** — "I built a bot. First 20 people to beat it get on the board." (best for chats/Discord)
2. **The learning angle** — "A fun way to practice loops & if-statements: write a bot that plays a game." (best for Reddit learn-to-code)
3. **The bragging artifact** — a shared replay link captioned "my bot won in 5 moves, beat that." (best for virality)

Pick the hook per channel below. Never lead with the tech stack.

---

## Phase 1 — Warm circles first (Week 1, target: first ~20 users)

Highest conversion, zero risk, builds the social proof you need before going public. **Never launch publicly to an empty leaderboard** — seed it here first.

1. **Your own class/cohort group chats & CS Discord/Slack.** Post the *dare*. Make it personal: "Spent the weekend building this — first person to beat my bot gets bragging rights, here's the link." DM 5–10 friends individually asking them to try it and tell you where they got stuck. Personal asks convert ~10x better than broadcasts.
2. **Your university CS subreddit / department channel.** Same dare, slightly more polished, with one screenshot or a 15-second clip of a match playing out.
3. **Goal of week 1:** ~20 signups and, critically, written feedback on where people rage-quit. These 20 become your leaderboard's initial "names to beat."

---

## Phase 2 — Learn-to-code communities (Week 2, target: ~50 cumulative)

Where beginners actively look for "what should I build / how do I practice." Lead with the **learning angle**, not the product. One genuine post per community, spaced a few days apart, and reply to *every* comment — engagement is the algorithm.

- **r/learnprogramming** — "I made a thing where you write a bot to play a game — surprisingly good way to practice control flow. Would love beginner feedback." (Framed as sharing, not promoting.)
- **r/Python, r/javascript** — language-specific: "Practice <language> by coding a game bot — here's mine."
- **r/cscareerquestions** project-share / "what are you building" threads.
- **The Odin Project / freeCodeCamp / CS50 Discord & forums** — these are *packed* with your exact audience asking for project ideas. Share as a fun practice tool.
- **Dev Twitter/X & dev.to** — post a shared replay clip with the *bragging* hook + a "try to beat it" link.

**Rule:** read each community's self-promo rules; contribute value, don't drive-by drop links, or you'll get banned and burn the channel.

---

## Phase 3 — CS clubs & a seeded contest (Weeks 2–4, the 100-user accelerator)

Clubs are the single fastest path to 100 because they deliver users in **batches of 20–50**.

1. **DM CS club organizers at 5–10 universities.** Pitch: "I built a free, ready-to-run 'intro to bot programming' activity — students write a bot and compete on a live leaderboard. Want it for your next meetup? I'll help you run it." You're handing them free programming for their event; many will say yes.
2. **Run a "Beat the Bot" weekly challenge.** Leaderboard resets weekly; top name gets a pinned "champion" shout-out (and optionally a $5–10 gift card if you want a small spike — but bragging rights alone work for this audience). Recurring contests create return visits, which is what turns 100 signups into 100 *users*.
3. **TA / professor angle:** ask a friendly instructor to mention it as an optional fun exercise. One classroom mention = dozens of signups.

---

## Phase 4 — Public launch (Week 3+, only once the leaderboard looks alive)

Do this **after** Phases 1–3 have seeded real names and matches, so newcomers see a living contest, not a ghost town.

- **Show HN (Hacker News):** title sells the hook — *"Show HN: Write code to climb a TicTacToe bot leaderboard."* Post early-morning US time, be present in comments all day.
- **Product Hunt:** schedule for 12:01am PT; line up 10–15 of your Phase 1–3 users in advance to upvote/comment in the first hour (the only thing that moves PH ranking).
- **Reddit r/programming / r/coolgithubprojects** once you have a polished clip.

These are one-shot spikes — spend them only when the experience and leaderboard are ready to convert the traffic.

---

## Phase 5 — Compounding content (ongoing, low effort)

Set-and-forget assets that pull users in for months after posting:

- **One "I built a bot battle arena" write-up** on dev.to/Medium with a try-it link — ranks in search, gets reshared.
- **A 30-second screen recording** (write a bot → watch it win → see your name climb) reused across every channel above. Video out-converts text everywhere for this audience.
- **Shareable replay links as your viral loop:** make sharing a win frictionless and nudge users to post their best match. Every share is a free, credible ad with a built-in "beat this" challenge.

## Sequencing at a glance

| Week | Focus | Cumulative target |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | Warm circles, seed leaderboard | ~20 |
| 2 | Learn-to-code communities + start club DMs | ~50 |
| 3–4 | CS club events + weekly contest + public launch | ~100 |
| Ongoing | Content + replay-sharing loop | compounding |

---

## How to know it's working (the only 4 numbers that matter)

Track the funnel, not vanity totals:
1. **Visited → signed up** — if low, your landing pitch (the *dare*) isn't landing.
2. **Signed up → submitted a first bot** — if low, onboarding is too hard; this is the most common leak for beginners.
3. **Submitted once → came back a second time** — the real retention signal; the leaderboard/contest exists to move this.
4. **Replays shared per active user** — your virality coefficient; if it's near zero, push the share-your-win nudge harder.

**Decision rule:** don't spend distribution goodwill (HN/PH/clubs) until the warm-circle cohort shows healthy *signup → first-bot* conversion. If beginners sign up but never submit, fix onboarding before scaling traffic — otherwise you're pouring users into a leaky funnel.

---

## What NOT to do

- Don't buy ads — wrong move at 0–100 users; you have no funnel data yet and the audience lives in free communities.
- Don't launch on HN/Product Hunt to an empty leaderboard.
- Don't broadcast-spam; one genuine, engaged post per community beats ten drive-by links (and avoids bans).
- Don't lead with the tech stack or feature list — lead with the dare.
